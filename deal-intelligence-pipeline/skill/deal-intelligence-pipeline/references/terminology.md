# Terminology

## Closed Deal

A completed or historical deal used as the source of truth for reusable deal intelligence.

A closed deal can contribute:
- validated deal context;
- answers to Nick's 3 questions;
- verified signals;
- evidence-backed examples of what mattered;
- warning signs and proof requirements;
- technical, commercial, stakeholder, adoption, or risk patterns.

A closed deal does **not** automatically prescribe what to do in another deal.

## Current Deal

An active deal we are trying to close now.

The system may compare a current deal against approved closed-deal intelligence, but the current deal's own evidence is the only basis for stating what is true about that current deal.

## Source of Truth

For reusable historical intelligence, approved closed deals are the source of truth.

For claims about the current deal, current-deal evidence is the source of truth.

This distinction prevents historical context from being copied into a current deal as if it were fact.

## Signal

An evidence-backed pattern extracted from a closed deal that preserves:
- the observed condition;
- the original deal context;
- the impact in that deal;
- the evidence;
- the confidence level;
- the circumstances in which the pattern may be relevant elsewhere.

A signal is not a universal rule.

## Candidate Signal

A signal proposed by analysis but not yet verified and approved.

Candidate signals may not be used for matching or the 5-Minute Read.

## Approved Signal

A candidate signal that:
- passed verification;
- has adequate supporting evidence;
- survived human review;
- is stored in the approved deal library.

## Pending Question

A direct question surfaced when a specific missing current-deal fact is required to validate a conclusion or match.

Example:

`Pending Question: Who owns the final expansion decision?`

The system must ask a pending question instead of inventing the missing answer.

## Match

A proposed relationship between a current deal and one or more approved closed-deal patterns based on relevant shared conditions.

A match is not proof that the current deal will behave the same way.
