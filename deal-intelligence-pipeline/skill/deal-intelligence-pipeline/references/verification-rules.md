# Verification Rules

Verification must challenge the analysis rather than rephrase it.

## For Every Candidate Signal or Major Conclusion, Check

1. Is the source legitimate and approved?
2. Is the underlying fact explicitly supported?
3. Is interpretation being presented as fact?
4. Is correlation being confused with causation?
5. Is there later-outcome evidence supporting the claimed impact?
6. Is there contradictory evidence?
7. Is the conclusion too generic to be useful?
8. Could another explanation fit the evidence equally well?
9. Is the conclusion relying on a different deal to fill a gap?
10. Is the conclusion dependent on a missing fact that should become a pending question?

## Verification Status

### Approved
Use when:
- the evidence supports the conclusion;
- no material contradiction remains unresolved;
- the interpretation is appropriately bounded;
- the signal preserves its original deal context.

### Needs Evidence
Use when:
- the conclusion is plausible but not sufficiently supported;
- later-outcome evidence is missing;
- source conflict remains unresolved;
- a material fact is absent.

### Rejected
Use when:
- the conclusion is unsupported;
- it depends on invented or assumed facts;
- it confuses activity with progression;
- it overstates causality;
- it is too generic to be useful;
- the evidence contradicts it.

## Confidence Standard

### High
- directly stated or strongly evidenced;
- supported by later outcome where causality / movement is claimed;
- little or no unresolved contradiction.

### Medium
- strongly implied;
- evidence is incomplete but materially supportive;
- interpretation remains bounded.

### Low
- partial evidence;
- important inference required;
- missing current or historical context;
- meaningful contradiction unresolved.

Low-confidence items should not automatically become approved reusable signals.
