## Nick's 3 Questions

### Q1: What actually moved it?
- **Answer:** The Feb 18 pilot acceptance. It set a concrete 90-day commitment with a day-75 decision point. Later outcome: the April attribution shift and the July ROI readout, which led to contract redlines and then per-property booking.
- **Supporting evidence:** Feb 18 transcript (pilot terms accepted); Apr 29 and Jul 22 (attribution results).
- **Later-outcome evidence:** Property bookings Sep 16 to 30; billing at 27 properties by Oct 16.
- **Weak signals excluded:** "A dream scenario" (Feb 18) and enthusiasm about suppression. Positive feedback alone is not movement.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high.

### Q2: What did the rep know going in, and was it dig-for? (PROPOSED REVISION, needs approval)
- **Previous status:** Unknown (Phase 3).
- **Proposed answer:** Discovery left the executive sponsor and budget authority unconfirmed. The Jan 14 discovery questions asked who approves the budget. The answer came in the demo: David (CIO) approves vendor paperwork ("That'll go to me," Jan 14). The same call surfaced cost sensitivity: David said $786k/year would need to come down.
- **Dig factor:** Required digging. The sponsor was not stated in advance.
- **Confidence:** Medium.
- **Unreconciled:** David's own arithmetic was garbled in the transcript. $290 x 210 properties x 12 months = $730,800, not $786k. The figure implies about $312/property/month.

### Q3: Why did it stall, and would a brief have caught it?
- **Stall symptom:** The pilot succeeded and billing began, but expansion from 35 properties toward the 250-property target has not moved.
- **Underlying reasons:**
  1. Price anchored on rack rate and the CIO's $786k calculation. The client's stated budget is well below the anchor.
  2. Rollout size: the client estimates 40 to 50 properties (Jun 15), not 250.
  3. Approval path: signer and approver were unnamed in July and October; the economic buyer was absent from the July expansion session.
- **Earlier warning signs:** Jan 14 cost objection; Mar 25 "not budgeted"; Jun 15 "not in the same ballpark" as PERC; Jul 22 absence of the economic buyer.
- **Brief catchability:** Partially. A brief would have flagged the price gap and the missing approver. It would not have fixed the product gap.
- **Confidence:** Medium.
